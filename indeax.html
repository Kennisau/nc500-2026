01

Welcome to Scotland

Saturday, October 3

Landing at Inverness Airport in the late afternoon, the journey begins with a smooth pickup of the rental car before heading northwest into the quiet countryside of Ross-shire. The drive to Contin is short and scenic, with October's early evening light casting a soft glow over the surrounding hills — a calm, easy start to the week ahead.

✈️
Land in Inverness

Touchdown in the Scottish Highlands.

🚗
Drive to Contin

A scenic 35-minute drive through the countryside.

🔥
Fireside evening

Settle in with a dram by the fire after a long flight.

Fun factScotland runs 5 hours ahead of the US East Coast — Highland dinner time lines up with mid-afternoon back home.
🛏️
Overnight: Coul House HotelAn elegant 18th-century country house set in secluded grounds.
02

The Wild West Coast & Bealach na Bà

Sunday, October 4

Today's drive tackles one of Britain's most dramatic roads: Bealach na Bà, or "Pass of the Cattle." Tight hairpin turns climb to over 2,000 feet before opening onto sweeping views across the sea to the Isle of Skye. The descent leads to the coastal hamlet of Applecross for a lunch of ultra-fresh, locally sourced food, then it's north along the coast into the glacial wilderness of Glen Torridon, where ancient sandstone peaks glow in autumn rust and copper tones.

🌀
Bealach na Bà

The steepest mountain road climb in the UK.

🐟
Applecross Inn

Lunch by the sea with fresh, local ingredients.

🍂
Glen Torridon

Ancient peaks lit up in autumn color.

Fun factBealach na Bà was built nearly 200 years ago — long before cars, it was used as a drovers' road to walk cattle over the mountain to market.
🛏️
Overnight: Shieldaig LodgeA Victorian hunting lodge on the edge of Loch Gairloch.
03

Assynt Landscapes & Atmospheric Ruins

Monday, October 5

North through dramatic geology, past the plunge-pool gorge at Corrieshalloch, to the eerie beauty of Loch Assynt. Here, the 15th-century ruins of Ardvreck Castle sit mirrored in dark water beneath moody autumn skies. A short optional walk leads to Wailing Widow Falls, where water cascades through a sheer limestone canyon. From there, the sweeping Kylesku Bridge carries the route to a characterful, owner-run harbourside inn for the night.

🌉
Corrieshalloch Gorge

A dramatic plunge-pool gorge carved by rushing water.

🏰
Ardvreck Castle

15th-century ruins reflected in a moody loch.

💦
Wailing Widow Falls

A waterfall crashing through a limestone canyon.

Fun factArdvreck Castle has its own ghost story — local legend tells of a lonely daughter said to still haunt the ruins.
🛏️
Overnight: Kylesku HotelA harbourside inn known for fresh, locally caught seafood.
04

Northern Coast to Orkney

Tuesday, October 6

The morning route crosses the rugged northern rim of the mainland toward Durness, stopping at Smoo Cave — a cavernous limestone sea cave lit by natural skylights and fed by an underground waterfall — before browsing handmade chocolates at Cocoa Mountain in Balnakeil Craft Village. The road continues along the dramatic Caithness coastline past the golden sweep of Strathy Beach. By late afternoon, it's on to Scrabster Harbour to board the NorthLink Ferry, a 90-minute crossing to Orkney with twilight views of the sea stack known as the Old Man of Hoy.

🕳️
Smoo Cave

A vast sea cave with a hidden underground waterfall.

🍫
Cocoa Mountain

Handmade chocolates at Balnakeil Craft Village.

⛴️
Ferry to Orkney

A 90-minute crossing past the Old Man of Hoy sea stack.

Fun factThe Old Man of Hoy is a 450-foot sea stack — taller than most skyscrapers — standing alone just off Orkney's coast.
🛏️
Overnight: The Lynnfield Hotel, KirkwallA classic country lodge next to Highland Park Distillery, home for two nights.
05

Heart of Neolithic Orkney & Grey Seals

Wednesday, October 7

A full day dedicated to Orkney's UNESCO World Heritage sites: the massive standing stones of the Ring of Brodgar and nearby Stones of Stenness, the 5,000-year-old subterranean village of Skara Brae, and the chambered tomb of Maeshowe. In the afternoon, the cliffs at Birsay and Point of Buckquoy come alive with Orkney's grey seal colony — October marks peak pupping season, with mother seals and pups along the rocky coves. The day wraps with a tasting at Highland Park Distillery and dinner in Kirkwall.

🪨
Ring of Brodgar

A massive neolithic stone circle.

🏺
Skara Brae

A 5,000-year-old stone village, older than the pyramids.

🦭
Birsay Seals

Grey seal pups on the rocks during pupping season.

Fun factSkara Brae was already ancient by the time the Great Pyramid of Giza or Stonehenge were built.
🛏️
Overnight: The Lynnfield Hotel, KirkwallSecond night in the same island hotel.
06

Orkney Heritage & Return to Mainland

Thursday, October 8

The final Orkney morning starts at the Italian Chapel on Lamb Holm — a beautifully hand-crafted sanctuary built from Nissen huts by Italian prisoners of war during WWII. From there, a stroll through Kirkwall takes in the towering red sandstone of St Magnus Cathedral and the town's craft galleries. By late afternoon, the return ferry carries the route back to Scrabster, with a possible sunset detour to Dunnet Head — the northernmost point of mainland Britain.

⛪
The Italian Chapel

A chapel hand-built from scrap metal huts during WWII.

🏛️
St Magnus Cathedral

A towering red-sandstone cathedral in Kirkwall.

🌅
Dunnet Head

The northernmost point of mainland Britain.

Fun factThe Italian Chapel was built almost entirely from salvaged materials — two Nissen huts, leftover paint, and scrap metal.
🛏️
Overnight: Forss House HotelA secluded 17th-century country house along the River Forss.
07

Wolfburn Distillery & Coastal Caithness to Brora

Friday, October 9

The morning begins at Wolfburn, mainland Scotland's northernmost distillery, with a look inside the still house and maturation warehouse followed by a guided tasting. From there, the route follows the dramatic east coast of Sutherland down to Dunrobin Castle — the fairytale-turreted ancestral seat of the Earls of Sutherland, with formal French-inspired gardens overlooking the Moray Firth.

⚙️
Wolfburn Distillery

A hands-on look at traditional whisky-making.

🏯
Dunrobin Castle

Fairytale turrets and formal gardens on the coast.

Fun factDunrobin Castle has around 189 rooms — more than most stately homes in Scotland.
🛏️
Overnight: Royal Marine HotelA cozy coastal hotel in Brora.
08

Dornoch Firth, Distilleries & the Black Isle

Saturday, October 10

A morning drive through the Black Isle leads to Chanonry Point, one of the best places in Europe to spot wild bottlenose dolphins feeding close to shore as the tide turns. The route loops back into Inverness for a final night, capped with a farewell dinner and a tasting flight at The Malt Room, a hidden, wood-panelled whisky bar in the city centre.

🐬
Chanonry Point

One of Europe's best spots for wild bottlenose dolphins.

🥃
The Malt Room

A tucked-away whisky bar for a farewell tasting.

Fun factThe bottlenose dolphins in the Moray Firth are among the largest in the world, thanks to the cold water.
🛏️
Overnight: Rocpool Reserve HotelA stylish final-night stay in Inverness.

Depart
